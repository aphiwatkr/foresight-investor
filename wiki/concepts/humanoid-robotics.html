Humanoid Robotics & Physical AI

Definition: General-purpose, human-form robots (and the "physical AI" stack that makes them
work) — the humanoid OEMs (Tesla Optimus, Figure, Agility, UBTech, Unitree, AgiBot), the
actuator / sensor / compute supply chain (strain-wave gears, motors, lidar, GPUs), and the
foundation models that give them dexterity. Split out of automation-robotics on 2026-09-07
when the theme cleared the frontier-watch promotion threshold (4-axis score 3.5): the
industry entered early commercialization in 2025–26 and liquid, dedicated ETFs now exist.
Conviction 2.5 on the trend-radar — a real S-curve inflection, but hype-heavy,
China-led, and priced for perfection at the edges.

Research/insight only — decisions for the operator's own book, never advice to others. Figures
tagged (fact)/(est.)/(inference) with period + source. "n/f" = no reliable figure found.
Market caps are momentum-driven and move fast — re-verify before use.

1. Definition & scope

(a) Humanoid OEMs — Tesla (Optimus, internal deployment), Figure (private), Agility (Digit,
private), Apptronik (private), UBTech (9880.HK), Unitree (STAR Market, listed 2026-08-19), AgiBot
(private); (b) actuators & precision components — Harmonic Drive Systems (6324.T), Nidec
(6594.T), Leader Harmonious Drive, Allient; (c) perception & sensing — Hesai (HSAI),
RoboSense, Hexagon; (d) compute & models — NVIDIA (Isaac / GR00T), the embodied-AI labs.
Distinct from automation-robotics (fixed industrial robots, cobots, motion control — a
cash-generative, cyclical business); this page is the general-purpose frontier where the
unit economics are not yet proven. Feeds on ai-software-agents (the "brain") and
semiconductors (the compute); competes for the same labor-substitution budget as
reshoring-supply-chain.

3. Key structural drivers

Demand: labor scarcity and rising wages in manufacturing/logistics (reshoring-supply-chain);
a pre-built form factor that fits existing human workspaces (no retooling); robotics-as-a-service
contracts (Agility Digit at Amazon/GXO: 100,000+ totes moved by Nov 2025 — Agility via Cervo,
(fact)); Chinese industrial policy (humanoids named a strategic industry). Supply / enablers:
vision-language-action foundation models (the ai-software-agents spill-over); NVIDIA's
simulation + compute stack (semiconductors); falling actuator costs; a Chinese supply chain that
already controls ~63% of humanoid components (Morgan Stanley 2025 via Cervo, (est.)).
Headwinds: unproven unit economics and uptime; dexterity/manipulation still the bottleneck;
safety certification for human-proximate work; export controls cutting both ways; and a
speculative bid — Unitree rose 542% on its Shanghai debut (CNBC, 2026-08-19, (fact)).

4. Value-chain / linkage

Rare earths & magnets (critical-minerals: MP Materials, Lynas — both KOID holdings) → motors
& strain-wave gears (Nidec, Harmonic Drive) → sensors (Hesai, RoboSense, Hexagon) → compute
(NVIDIA, semiconductors) → embodied-AI models (ai-software-agents) → humanoid OEMs (Tesla,
Figure, UBTech, Unitree) → deployment in logistics/auto plants (reshoring-supply-chain,
automation-robotics customers). Downstream it is a labor substitute, so it is a long-run
tension edge for wage-driven EM manufacturing (asean-thailand, india-em-growth) — see
theme-linkage-map.

5. Competitive landscape

Fragmented and China-led on volume: Unitree (~5,500 units, ~32% share) and AgiBot (~5,200)
shipped more in 2025 than every Western OEM combined; Figure (350+ Figure 03 by Apr 2026), Tesla
(~150 Optimus in 2025 per Omdia; 1,000+ Gen 3 internal in 2026 per press — (est.)), Agility
(~250 Digit) and Apptronik (~80) are Western pilots (all via Cervo, 2026-07-17). Moats today are
prospective — data flywheels from fleet deployments, actuator IP (Harmonic Drive's strain-wave
patents are the one established moat), and access to compute. The supply chain is the safer
seat: component makers sell to every OEM regardless of who wins, which is why both ETFs are
weighted toward suppliers rather than OEMs. Private leaders (Figure at a $39B Series C, Sep 2025)
keep the purest exposure off public markets. (inference)

7. Competitiveness read

The theme has moved from prototype to early commercialization: real shipments (13–18k in
2025), real RaaS revenue (Agility), a real IPO (Unitree), and forecasts of 50–90k units in 2026.
But China owns the volume and most of the supply chain, the Western leaders (Figure, Agility)
are private, and Tesla's Optimus is a tiny option inside a $1.4T company — so the investable
public exposure is diluted by construction. That is why both ETFs are supply-chain-heavy: KOID's
top-10 is Hexagon, Rainbow Robotics, Magna, MP Materials, NVIDIA, TE Connectivity, Amphenol,
Nidec, Keyence, Allient (KraneShares, 2026-09-04) — sensors, motors, magnets, connectors, compute.
The safer money is in the components (Harmonic Drive, Nidec, MP/Lynas via
critical-minerals); the OEMs are lottery tickets with a 542%-debut sentiment tape. Net: a
legitimate frontier worth a small, ETF-expressed sleeve — sized like space-economy, not like
automation-robotics. (inference)

8. Risks / what kills the thesis

  • Unit economics never close — $90–100k pilot BOM vs. a labor-cost break-even that needs
    <$30k and >85% uptime; if 2026 shipments print well below the 50k+ forecasts, the S-curve claim fails.
  • Dexterity / safety wall — manipulation and human-proximate safety certification slip years.
  • Hype unwind — Unitree +542% debut, UBTech +104% growth on tiny revenue; a risk-off tape
    (macro-regime) hits this cluster first (see theme-linkage-map speculative cluster).
  • Purity dilution — the ETFs are ~30% China, supplier-heavy, and young ($333M / $94M AUM); a
    fund closure or index rejig changes the exposure overnight.
  • Geopolitics — export controls on compute (NVIDIA) or rare earths (China) can sever the chain
    in either direction.
  • Incumbent capture — NVIDIA/Tesla/BYD-scale players may take the value, leaving OEM equity
    holders with the losses (the space-economy / SpaceX pattern).

10. Our view — conviction / stage / horizon

  • Conviction 2.5/5 — the adoption curve has genuinely inflected (3–5× unit growth, first
    commercial RaaS, first pure-play IPO) and there is finally a clean, liquid vehicle. Held to 2.5
    because unit economics are unproven, the volume and supply chain are China-led (geopolitical +
    purity risk), the Western leaders are private, and the sentiment tape is euphoric. A small
    speculative sleeve alongside space-economy; the portfolio-blueprint speculative bucket
    cap applies.
  • Stage: emerging (early commercialization). Horizon: 5–10 years to mass adoption; 1–2 years to
    know whether 2026's 50k+ forecast was real.
  • Invalidation: the published 2026 global shipment count (Omdia/IDC/TrendForce) comes in
    < 25,000 units (below half the low forecast), OR KOID + HUMN combined AUM falls < $200M
    (the liquid vehicle fails), OR a flagship program (Optimus external sales, Figure) is cancelled.
  • Sensors: P30 (2026 shipments ≥ 40k) and P31 (KOID vs BOTZ, 12m) in prediction-ledger.
  • Frontier-watch score at promotion (2026-09-07): adoption 3 · substance 3 · purity 4 ·
    time-to-liquid 4 = 3.5 (threshold ≥3.5, no axis = 1). Borderline — re-score at the quarterly REVIEW.
