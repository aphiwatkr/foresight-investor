100x Anatomy — What the Last Decade's Winners Looked Like at the Base

Bottom line: We mined our own 10-year price cache and SEC XBRL filings for every equity that
did ≥20x over 2016-10 → 2026-10 (22 winners; NVDA 139x is the only equity that cleared 100x
full-span — MU did 60x, not 100x), plus 15 controls. Four findings survive contact with the
data: (1) every measurable winner was machine-detectably at its base at the trough — the
base is real and findable; (2) at the base you cannot see the wave — trough-year financials
of winners are indistinguishable from controls (median rev growth 6.2% vs 6.4%, 8/19 loss-making,
gross margins lower than controls); (3) the inflection shows up within ~12 months of the
base (launch-year growth: median +17%, 16/17 positive) — so "base + first confirmed improvement"
is the actionable window and waiting for it is cheap; (4) half the cohort would have failed
our own liquidity gate at the trough (LEU $0.1M ADV, CLS $3.9M) and several rode leverage and
dilution, not net cash — two Gate-0 rules need surgery. The point-in-time replay is brutally
honest: the price-side layers alone have zero edge (2018–2022 starts); the only year-start with
full factors (2023) screened 8/15 ≥3x vs a 17.7% base rate — n=1, not proof. The screen is a
candidate generator; the memo pipeline remains the selector. This is the anatomy behind the
now-built scripts/hundredx_screen.py (hundred-x-hunt-framework's standing offer, delivered).

3. What the anatomy shows — seven findings

F1 · The base is real and machine-detectable. At every one of the 10 troughs with ≥200d of
prior price history, the screen's price layers (within 40% of 2y low + ≥30% under the high) flag
10/10. For the 9 mid-window bases the from-low multiple beat buy-anywhere by a median ~2×
(QUBT 13×, CLS 3.8×, ENPH 4.2×, LEU 2.7×); window-start bases were already post-crash, so full-span
≈ from-low for them (cohort medians 35x vs 45x). Buying at the base isn't everything — but it is
the only part of the 100x you control.

F2 · At the base you cannot see the wave. Trough-year winners vs controls: median rev growth
+6.2% vs +6.4% — identical. 8/19 winners were loss-making at the base (controls 5/10 — no
difference). Median gross margin: winners 23% vs controls 44% — winners were less margin-rich
(the hardware/manufacturing path dominates this cohort). Any screen that demands visible growth,
profitability, or high margins at the base excludes the cohort.

F3 · The inflection is visible within ~12 months — and that's the window. Launch-year
(base+1) revenue growth: median +17.2%, 16/17 positive with data (MU +64%, NVDA +41%, ANET
+46%, LRCX +38%, AVGO +33%; the one flat name is CLS at −2%). Combined with F2: the actionable
template is at-base + first confirmed improvement, not "buy the chart low." The cost of waiting
for the first improving print is small against a 20–100x — and it's exactly what our memo
pipeline's print-confirmation tranches already do.

F4 · "Funded" ≠ net cash — funded = can finance the inflection. 12/19 winners had net cash at
base vs 4/10 controls (suggestive, not decisive). But TSLA rode −$4.1B net debt, KLAC −$1.9B,
VRT −$1.6B. And the Soros-reflexive-fuel mark shows up as dilution into the base: TSLA
+434% shares over the two years to its base, VKTX +270%, VRT +160%, ENPH +81%, CIEN +43% — while
MU, KLAC, PWR bought back 6–14%. Both directions work; what matters is surviving to the
inflection. Gate-0's "net cash" rule should read "runway to the inflection" (net cash, self-funding,
or demonstrated market access — with dilution-as-fuel explicitly named as acceptable).

F5 · "GM ≥40%" is a software-path filter that would have excluded half the winners. CLS 7.6%,
PWR 15%, TSLA 17%, MU 20%, AMD 23%, ENPH 20%, DELL 22%. The moat mark (P2) must split by business
model: software/services path keeps the GM≥40% test; the hardware/capacity path substitutes
technology/process moat + cycle position (MU at −23% growth and −2% margin was the memory-cycle
base). Margin expansion after the base is where the hardware path pays (CLS 7.6%→GM now
multiplied; MU 20%→supercycle re-rating).

F6 · Caps at the base are tiny — where measurable. Sanity-gated estimates: LEU ~$14M, VKTX
~$37M, ENPH ~$59M, FIX ~$950M, VRT ~$1.7B, CIEN ~$2.9B, PWR ~$3.4B, AMD ~$5.3B, MU ~$16.8B (MU
estimate carries share-scope risk). Most names stay n/f — XBRL share counts mix scopes and
historical splits are unrecoverable from this endpoint; we publish only what survives internal
coherence checks (est. tagged, caveats in the JSON). Directionally consistent with the framework's
"<$3B typical start" and Mayer's <$500M median (b).

F7 · The liquidity gate would have kept us out of the winners. ADV at trough: LEU $0.1M,
VKTX $0.2M, QUBT ~$0M, CLS $3.9M, VRT $22M — six of ten measurable winners under the $25M Gate-0
line. The gate's true job is sleeve capacity (how much we can own), not selection (what to
study). It moves from a hard gate to a sizing tier: sub-$25M ADV names get the smallest tranche
allowance and a slippage note, not a rejection.

4. Winners vs controls — the discrimination test

Metric at base Winners (n) Controls (n) Discriminates?
Revenue growth (yoy) median +6.2% (16) +6.4% (9) No
Loss-making share 8/19 5/10 No
Gross margin median 23% (17) 44% (5) Inverted (hardware path)
Net cash > 0 12/19 4/10 Weakly yes
Launch-year growth median +17.2%, 16/17 > 0 (n/a — controls don't inflect by construction) Yes
Dilution into base mixed, median ~+4%/2y — Fuel, both directions

The honest read: almost nothing at the base discriminates — what discriminates is what happens
next. Which is exactly why the framework's qualitative marks (category error, monopoly-in-
disguise, operator quality) exist and why the memo pipeline, not the screen, is the selector.

Read this table as the price of honesty:

  • 2018–2022 starts: no edge. Top-15 hit rates sit at-or-below the gate-passer base rate;
    medians match the universe. Beaten-down small caps as a class mostly stay beaten down; the
    chart does not know which company will execute. (2022's start into the bear is the floor case.)
  • 2023 start: real hit-rate lift (53% ≥3x vs 17.7%; PLTR 27.7x, MSTR 10.7x, AFRM 7.7x, COIN
    6.4x, META 5.5x among the top-15) — but it is n=1 year, the only year-start where the
    growth/moat layers were computable, and it rode an epic bull. We claim directional support for
    the full-factor screen    , not a validated edge.
  • The replay also had no cap gate (caps are current-snapshot), so its top-15 includes
    mega-caps (META, BABA) that the forward book would gate out — another reason not to over-read it.

Winner self-test (F1) plus the replay give the mechanic its honest job description: find bases
(reliable), refuse to promise which base ignites (impossible from price alone), and feed
base-qualified candidates to the memo layer where the qualitative marks do the real work.

1 Category error Validated (not machine-checkable) Stays qualitative; it is the memo layer's #1 question
2 Small & unwatched Validated Cap floor confirmed where measurable (F6); obscurity stays manual
3 Inflects before price Corrected Growth at the base is NOT visible (F2); require launch-year inflection (F3). R&D ≥15% of sales holds where data exists (cohort ~13–24%)
4 Monopoly in disguise Split by path Software path: GM ≥40%. Hardware path: moat = technology/cycle position; GM 15–25% at base is normal (F5)
5 Operator + reflexive fuel Broadened Near-death washout is common but not necessary; equity fuel = dilution into the base is normal and healthy (F4)
6 Funded to the inflection Broadened Net cash OR self-funding OR demonstrated market access (F4); TSLA-style leverage passes only with owner-operator

7. The portfolio math nobody escapes (why the tranche stays small)

Base rate: 1 in ~349 tracked equities did ≥100x over the decade (NVDA; BTC/SOL aside as
non-equities). Unconditional per-decade equity rate ≈ 0.3%. If screening + memos concentrate
10× better than random (the 2023 replay's most generous read: 13% ≥10x vs 2.2% base), a
20-name tranche at 0.3–0.5% each:

Scenario Book impact (0.4% × 20 names)
0 hits, 60–80% round-trip to ~0 sleeve ≈ −4 to −6% of book over the decade — the known cost
1 × 100x ≈ +40% book — one name carries everything
1 × 100x + 2 × 10x ≈ +48% book
P(≥1 hit) at 1–3% per-name 18–45% — i.e. the most likely single outcome is no hit

The bet is an asymmetric option basket with negative-ish median and huge right tail — which is
why portfolio-blueprint caps it at 5–8% of book, why names size at 0.25–0.5%, and why the
coffee-can rule (never trim the winner early) is the part that actually generates the 100x
(Mayer: >50% of holders earn <10x on the same stock by trading it (b)).

8. The mechanic — what was built

  • scripts/fetch_anatomy_financials.py — cohort mining + SEC XBRL annual panel (winners +
    controls) → _hist/hundredx_anatomy_financials.json. Re-run quarterly for freshness.
  • scripts/hundredx_screen.py — Gate 0 (automated halves) + 5 pillar proxies + base detection →
    ranked candidate book (_hist/hundredx_screen.json + .md) with an explicit manual checklist
    (founder %, niche share, analyst count, category-error read). Late-rides (>200% above 2y low)
    listed separately per the framework's second-100x rule; pre-revenue names flagged for
    option-sizing.
  • --replay — the point-in-time validation above → _hist/hundredx_replay.json.
  • Integration: the screen becomes the screen of record for the 100x tranche in
    security-sourcing-pipeline (quarterly re-score + on-demand; output feeds the memo queue;
    decisions stay gated on operator go; tranche names keep being watched by MONITOR).

10. Confidence & gaps

  • The price cache is survivorship-biased (names that exist today); the true historical rate of
    100x includes the ones that died along the way — the replay base rates are therefore too kind.
  • Full-factor replay is n=1 year (fundamentals panel starts 2021-12). The 2026 start scores
    exist in the forward book; its 3y verdict lands 2029 — that is the real test, and it should be
    scored then (resolve-by date if we file a P-call on operator go).
  • XBRL gaps honestly left open: MRVL pre-2018 (entity reorg), 000660.KS/DELTA.BK (non-SEC),
    QUBT pre-revenue opacity, AVGO FY2015. Cap-at-base estimates carry share-scope risk and are
    order-of-magnitude only.
  • The qualitative marks (category error, monopoly, operator) remain human judgments — the machine
    finds the door; the memo decides whether to walk through it.
  • MU is a 60x, not a 100x: "learn from MU and NVIDIA" = one 100x + one 60x, both of which sat
    at machine-detectable bases with cycle-trough financials the screen could have flagged — and
    would have needed the memo layer to distinguish from every other beaten-down cyclical.
