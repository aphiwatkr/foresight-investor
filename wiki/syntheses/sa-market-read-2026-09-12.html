SA market read 2026-09-12 — policy hinge, war shock, hardware-over-software

Bottom line: The Seeking Alpha platform's live picture (2026-09-11/12) says the market has arrived at a policy hinge: a near-priced Fed hike (Sep 16) + BoJ the next day + oil at ~$110 + 10Y at ~5%. This is the exact cocktail our regime-monitor flagged as the late-Act-1 exit risk. For our own book the actionable reads are: (1) signal-monitor's 10Y warn threshold (>4.75%) is now breached at ~4.95% — the sensor panel is stale (July) and needs a MONITOR run; (2) SA's analyst community has turned hardware-over-software — the same rotation our FFM momentum sleeve shows — and is now bearish two names our model likes (NVO via MS downgrade, NuScale via UBS Sell); (3) the war premium in oil + copper's tariff wobble are fresh risks to the M2/M8 diversifier sleeves; (4) breadth (equal-weight 4th straight down week vs cap-weight up) is the quiet deterioration worth watching more than any single headline.

2. Hardware-over-software — SA converges with our momentum sleeve

The week's tape: gainers SWKS +23.6%, COHR +15.5%, HPE +14.0%, GLW +13.9%, AMD +13.2%; losers PTC −12.9%, FICO −11.9%, ADBE −11.7%, TYL −11.2%, ADSK −10.6%. The "Buy Hardware, Sell Software" thesis rests on a hard number: McKinsey — 32% of companies (41% in tech sector) stopped buying software last year, building it internally with agentic AI. Hardware, by contrast, has physical scarcity: hyperscaler 2026 capex ~$650B ($380B data centers), 24 GW announced vs ~12 GW actually energizable, GPU scarcity birthing neoclouds (NBIS). Bianco's frame is complementary: the AI buildout is inflationary now (construction, chips, power) with productivity payoffs deferred — one more reason the Fed's problem won't quietly vanish.

Our FFM already leans this way (top momentum = AI-capex cluster; the engine's concentration cap on that cluster exists for exactly this reason). The new information is (a) software weakness is now structural-narrative, not just rotation — Adobe beat and still fell 11.7% on ARR deceleration; (b) memory (MU, SNDK) is called the most volatile sub-sector — Deep Value explicitly describes rotation out of memory into servers on hot-CPI days. That's a nuance for our momentum sleeve's near-term risk: MU is the one name where SA-top-rated and FFM Strong Buy agree (84.5) — highest-conviction overlap, and also now the crowd's favorite volatility outlet.

4. Anthropic — the $200B S&P earnings ghost

Bianco's stat: Anthropic (30% owned by GOOGL+AMZN) has added ~$200B to S&P 500 earnings via its owners' stakes — more than any S&P member contributes directly. With the Anthropic IPO machinery in motion (NVDA as potential investor; JPM/BofA fighting to manage employee wealth), this is both (a) a concentration-risk data point for us-megacap-quality and (b) a prospective 2027 index-event. Also noted: Anthropic's own CEO called to "slow the pace" of model improvements, and an Anthropic+OpenAI alum published a high-profile AI-safety exit letter — the safety-vs-race narrative is escalating on both sides even as capex accelerates.
